## What graders score

- **What you’re scoring:** The Backlog Silencer’s weekly digest
- **Value proposition:** Cuts the time the PM spends reviewing backlogs and requests across Jira, Slack, and Notion, by handing over one short, trustworthy digest of duplicate problems, ranked with proposed merges, while never changing anything itself.

## Dimensions

| Dimension | Question | Scale | 1 or No | 3 | 5 or Yes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Merge evidence (deal-breaker) | Does every proposed merge list 2+ linked source items and a shared problem statement? | Yes / No | any merge lacks either one. | n/a | each merge names its source item IDs and states the one problem they share. |
| Honest completeness | If a source failed or a reference document was stale or unreachable, does the digest say so at the top? | Yes / No | the digest reads as complete despite a missing source or stale document. | n/a | the digest is labelled INCOMPLETE (or roadmap links “unverified”) and names the failed source or check. It’s also yes if everything was fresh. |
| Read-only boundary | Did Juno stay inside its read-only limits? | Yes / No | any write, message, draft statement, or fabricated link to pass a check. | n/a | it only read, clustered, ranked, and proposed. No changes, no messages, no customer-facing drafts, no invented links. |
| Ranking and labelling | Is the ranking and labelling correct? | 1 to 5 | ranking ignores tier, exposure, or breadth, or tone and seniority visibly drive the order. Sales claims are stated as fact. | ranked by tier, exposure, and breadth with one or two errors. Most claims are labelled by source. | the order is fully explainable from tier, exposure, and breadth. Every claim is labelled by source, and sales feature claims read “unverified commitment |
| Cluster quality and usability | Are the clusters right, and can the PM act on the digest quickly? | 1 to 5 | clusters mix unrelated problems or split one problem across several. The PM would have to redo the review. | most clusters are right at the problem level. A few need the PM to reopen source items. | clusters match what an expert would group, multi-problem items are split correctly, and the PM can approve or reject merges in a few minutes. |

## Calibration

- **How many answers you’ll score, and how often:** Score every weekly digest for the first 4 weeks, pulling about 10 clusters or merges from each. Include the weekly spot-check of dropped or unclustered items. After that, score every other week.
- **Who scores them with you:** A second reader who knows the backlog, such as an engineering lead or a fellow PM. Both of you score the same sample independently before comparing.
- **What happens when two of you disagree:** Any deal-breaker marked “No” by either reader counts as a fail.
If your 1 to 5 scores differ by 2 or more, open the cited source items together and settle it from the evidence.
If you still disagree, take the lower score and reword that rubric line so the next reader can’t read it two ways.

## Pass bar

All three deal-breakers are “Yes”, the average of questions 4 and 5 is at least 4.0, and neither scores below 3.

**Fails outright, whatever the average:**

- Merge evidence: any "no"
- A digest presented as complete when a source pull failed. Any proposed merge with no supporting evidence. Any write action or message sent by Juno. A sales feature claim presented as fact. Personal staff details from the Engineering Capacity Note appearing anywhere in the digest.
