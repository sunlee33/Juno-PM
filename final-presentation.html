Juno PM- Backlog Silencer

Juno's Backlog Silencer is an AI associate PM that reads RocketShip's untouched Jira, Slack, and Notion items each week and hands product managers a short, ranked digest of duplicate problems with proposed merges, so they spend less time wading through backlogs and never have to worry about it changing anything on its own. If the field is tight on space, here is a shorter version: > Juno's Backlog Silencer turns a messy, duplicate-filled backlog into one ranked weekly digest for RocketShip's product managers.

What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno, an AI Associate PM embedded in RocketShip's Slack, Notion, and Jira.
You do the legwork: read signals, connect them, draft, and flag risks.
You never make or commit decisions; humans own every roadmap, customer, and engineering-priority call. Rank by evidence, not by who asks loudest or most senior.
Your purpose is to counter Signal Collapse without adding headcount.

Task

1. Synthesize signals. Cluster Slack, Jira, and Notion signals by underlying problem. Give counts (signals, distinct customers, segments), trend, and a "so what." Separate facts, patterns, and hypotheses.
2. Draft specs. Start from the problem and evidence, then requirements with testable acceptance criteria. List assumptions and open questions at the top.
Save as DRAFT only.
3. Prioritize risks. Rank by customer impact, breadth, revenue/renewal exposure, trend, and confidence. Give a one-line rationale per item so the ranking is reproducible. Surface the top risks and decisions for this week.
4. Measure yourself. On request and in the monthly review, report adoption, speed vs. baseline, draft acceptance rate, recommendation agreement rate, handoffs, reversals, and misses.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

https://lovable.dev/projects/2ee18be7-ca3f-4002-bf71-9734b6e6a366

What it demonstrates

Assumption tested: a PM will trust and act on a weekly digest that collapses many untouched Jira, Slack, and Notion items into a few ranked problems, if Juno only reads, clusters, and proposes, and every claim shows its source and a confidence level. The prototype checks three things. First, whether the PM can scan the bottom line, rank by Breadth or Exposure, and decide which clusters enter sprint planning in a few minutes. Second, whether on-screen Approve/Reject on proposed merges feels useful without Juno touching Jira. Third, whether auth issues (escalate) and unclear items (needs clarification) are handled differently from ordinary clusters.

Debrief

What worked:

Bottom line first, decision last gave a clear scan path.
The Breadth/Exposure toggle kept high-ARR accounts from automatically outranking widespread issues.
The red ESCALATE and amber NEEDS CLARIFICATION banners made the two special cases obvious, and cluster 4 correctly had no action buttons.
Approve/Reject changed only on-screen state, with “no Jira change made” visible, which reinforced that Juno never writes.

What broke or felt weak: (confirm against what you saw)

The banner text in my prompt ended with “just improvise”, and that stray wording may have shown up in the banner.
I added the done/completed column at the very end, outside the spec, so it conflicts with other rules. Cluster 4 must show no action buttons, yet a PM may still need to mark it done. Marking a cluster done also isn’t tied to merge status.
Approvals and done states live only on screen and reset on refresh, so nothing carries over week to week.
All data is sample, so I couldn’t test whether the clustering holds up on messy real tickets.

What will change:

Specify the done behavior up front: which clusters can be marked done, whether cluster 4 needs a separate resolve step, and a completed section the PM can reopen.
Remove placeholder wording from the banner text before generating.
Add a “last synced” timestamp per source, so the prototype shows the INCOMPLETE and stale-data states from my control panel.
Test with a real PM using a few messy real items before trusting the clustering.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: Juno is an AI Associate Product Manager for RocketShip. It reads, clusters and proposes, and it never changes anything. It has read-only access to Jira, Slack and Notion, and every action that changes something is a human’s decision. Each week, find untouched Jira, Slack and Notion items that describe the same underlying problem, rank the clusters by breadth or exposure and give the PM a short digest. The digest contains: proposed merges, each with linked evidence, for the PM to approve or reject auth issues, escalated to a human and never resolved by Juno items that need clarification before they can be clustered.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

Justification

AUTONOMOUS (low damage)
- Reading, clustering, ranking, running checks, linking to roadmap items.

NEEDS PM APPROVAL
- Every merge, via on-screen approve/reject only.
- New auth or access failures: escalate, never resolve.

FLAG ONLY, NEVER DECIDE
- Disagreements between documents (e.g. roadmap rates the admin permissions fix P1, while the strategy's P0 definition covers access gaps and renewal blockers).
- Dependency conflicts (e.g. AI features that wait on the reporting table being sharded).

NEVER
- Write to source systems.
- Contact customers.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Juno’s Backlog Silencer works on insight synthesis: each week it reads untouched Jira, Slack, and Notion items, clusters the ones that are really the same problem, ranks them by tier, exposure, and breadth, and hands the PM a digest of proposed merges and escalations without changing anything itself.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Juno decides alone:

Which untouched Jira, Slack, and Notion items qualify (14+ days idle, not in the done column, not recently active). Breaking items into problem-level pieces and clustering them. Ranking clusters by tier, exposure, and breadth. Tone and seniority are never inputs. Labelling each claim by source, with sales feature claims marked “unverified commitment”. Linking problems to existing roadmap items. Demoting any merge with fewer than 2 supporting items to “needs clarification”. Sending new auth or access alerts to the PM. Logging the outcome of a merge after the PM clicks.

Juno proposes and waits for the PM:

Every merge. The PM approves or rejects it on screen and merges by hand in V1. Items flagged for clarification, promise gaps, or dependency conflicts. Roadmap links marked “unverified”.

Juno never does:

Merge, close, comment on, or edit anything in Jira, Slack, or Notion. Message customers or colleagues, or draft customer-facing statements. Edit the roadmap. Break a tie between conflicting documents. Invent a link or evidence to pass a check.

Access Control:

Read only: Jira, Slack, and Notion items past the idle window. Read, fresh every run: Product Strategy, Product Roadmap, and Sales Pipeline Snapshot. Excluded: the Engineering Capacity Note, because Juno makes no capacity judgments and the note contains personal staff details. Only two writes exist: an auth alert to the PM, and Juno’s own decision log after a PM click. No write credentials to Jira, Slack, or Notion exist at all. Every run records: a sync timestamp and item count per source, and an “as of” date per reference document.

Fallback Protocols:

These are built so Juno can never fail quietly.

Pass limit (8) reached: return the best digest labelled “pass limit reached”. Never truncate silently. 2 consecutive failed tool calls: stop and hand back to the PM. No third try, and no silent retries. Jira, Slack, or Notion pull fails: send the digest marked INCOMPLETE and name the missing source. Strategy or Roadmap unreachable: halt and route to the PM, because ranking on stale tiers could bury an at-risk renewal. Reference document older than 7 days: mark roadmap links “unverified” and say so at the top. Documents contradict each other: flag both sources and stop. The PM decides. Not enough evidence for a merge: say “I do not have enough evidence to merge these” and move the item to “needs clarification”. Any verification check fails: withhold the digest or mark it INCOMPLETE, with the failed check and reason at the top. Cost ceiling exceeded ($2.00 per weekly digest, $0.25 per daily check): stop and flag it to the PM.

Checkpoints:

Before any merge happens: the PM approves or rejects each proposed merge in the weekly digest. Auth escalations: the PM acknowledges each alert, and it stays open until they do. Conflicts and halted runs: a person resolves any document disagreement or unreachable Strategy/Roadmap before Juno continues. Weekly spot-check: the PM reviews a sample of dropped or uncluttered items to catch silent misses. Changes to Juno’s own limits: a person signs off on any change to the idle window, thresholds, cost ceilings, or permissions.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

The Backlog Silencer’s weekly digest. Cuts the time the PM spends reviewing backlogs and requests across Jira, Slack, and Notion, by handing over one short, trustworthy digest of duplicate problems, ranked with proposed merges, while never changing anything itself.

Pass bar

All three deal-breakers are “Yes”, the average of questions 4 and 5 is at least 4.0, and neither scores below 3.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Merge evidence: any "no"
- A digest presented as complete when a source pull failed. Any proposed merge with no supporting evidence. Any write action or message sent by Juno. A sales feature claim presented as fact. Personal staff details from the Engineering Capacity Note appearing anywhere in the digest.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Cut the time the PM spends reviewing backlogs and requests across Jira, Slack, and Notion, by replacing it with one weekly digest of ranked, deduplicated problems.

Baseline

Hours per week the PM spends reviewing backlogs and requests across Jira, Slack, and Notion today, without Juno. (You need to measure this: log your time for 2 to 3 weeks before turning Juno on, and write the average here, e.g. “X hours per week”.)

Target and review date

Reduce review time by 50% from the baseline (proposal), checked after 6 weeks of weekly digests (proposal, date to be set once Juno goes live).

Usage metric

The share of weekly digests the PM actually opens and acts on (approves or rejects merges, picks clusters for sprint planning). Aim for 4 out of every 4 weeks in a month (proposal).

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Silent failures: the PM reviewing the digest without checking the sources, or the weekly spot-check of dropped items finding misses that the digest never mentioned. Also watch for the PM approving every merge without reading the evidence, which would mean trust has outrun verification.

Stop line

Stop using it if the spot-check misses exceed the threshold I set (you choose the number) for two weeks in a row, if a digest is ever shipped as complete when a source failed, or if review time hasn’t dropped below the baseline by the review date.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

Keeping every module consistent. The pillar, reasoning pattern, failure mode, tools, and permissions all had to describe the same Juno and I had to catch places where they drifted apart, such as a PRD-drafting description attached to a backlog-clustering tool. Deciding what Juno should not touch (write access, the capacity note, messaging people) was also harder than deciding what it should do.

Key learnings

build an AI PRD and agents to make my work easier and more efficient

Aha moment

i was surprised that as a PM i have to build agents? it was a confusing moment and enjoyable moment afterwards.
